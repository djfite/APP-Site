# Legal Content Checklist — Privacy Policy & Terms and Conditions

## Privacy Policy checklist

### Scope and identity

Who you are (APP Merchant Services, Palm Coast, FL), what the site is for, and that it's directed at US businesses, not consumers or patients. Effective date and a "last updated" line.

A clear statement that the site markets payment processing services to businesses and that you collect business contact information, not patient or medical information.

### The healthcare-adjacent section (your differentiator, do this well)

Explicit statement: you are not a healthcare provider, pharmacy, or HIPAA covered entity, and the site is not intended to collect protected health information. Instruct visitors to never submit patient names, prescription details, or medical information through any form on the site.

What happens if PHI is submitted anyway: you'll delete it. (State it, then actually do it.)

Note that merchant underwriting documents (licenses, bank statements, IDs) are NOT collected through the website, and forms should never be used to send card numbers, SSNs, or account credentials. This protects you and sets up the secure-channel handoff.

### What you actually collect

Form data: name, business name, email, phone, and the business-model/licensing answers in the pharmacy pre-qual form. Be specific that pre-qual answers describe the business, not patients.

Automatically collected data: cookies, IP address, device/browser info, pages visited, via Google Analytics and Cloudflare (including Cloudflare's bot-detection cookies). Name the vendors: Jotform, Google Analytics, Cloudflare, Hostinger, Meta (if you ever add a pixel), Google Workspace for email.

### How it's used and shared

Purposes: responding to inquiries, evaluating pre-qualification, marketing follow-up, improving the site.

The one disclosure unique to your business: information submitted through pre-qualification or contact forms may be shared with your payment processing partners and their sponsor banks for the purpose of evaluating eligibility and establishing a merchant account. You can say this categorically without naming bluu or Merrick, consistent with your positioning. This disclosure is not optional; it's the entire point of the form.

Whether you "sell" or "share" data under state-law definitions. You don't sell. But be careful: sending data to ad platforms constitutes "sharing" under state laws, so if you ever add a Meta pixel or run retargeting, the answer changes and the policy must too. For now, "we do not sell personal information and do not share it for targeted advertising" is likely accurate and is a strong trust line for this audience.

No sharing with third parties for their own marketing.

### Rights, cookies, and mechanics

A consumer rights section: access, correction, deletion, and opt-out of marketing. Honestly, you're under every state threshold (the common trigger is controlling data on 100,000 consumers, with Rhode Island as low as 35,000), so most of the 20 state laws don't technically bind you. Best practice is to offer the rights anyway and say you honor requests from residents of any state; it costs you nothing at your volume and reads as credibility to compliance-minded pharmacy buyers.

Cookie disclosure and how to control cookies. Also decide your position on Global Privacy Control: 11 or more states now require businesses to treat GPC browser signals as valid opt-out requests. Since you're not selling or sharing data, you can state that GPC signals are moot for your site because there's nothing to opt out of, but only if that stays true.

Email/communications consent: submitting a form constitutes consent to be contacted by email or phone about the inquiry; every marketing email includes unsubscribe (CAN-SPAM). If you ever add SMS follow-up, you need a separate express-consent checkbox on the form (TCPA); don't bury it in the policy.

Data retention (how long you keep form submissions and chat logs; pick a real number and follow it), security measures in plain language, breach notification commitment (breach notification laws exist in all 50 states regardless of business size).

Children: site not directed at anyone under 18; you don't knowingly collect minors' data.

Third-party links disclaimer, policy-change process, and a contact method for privacy requests (your derek@ email is fine).

## Terms & Conditions checklist

### Who you are and what the site is (the section that matters most for you)

Acceptance of terms by using the site.

Service description: APP Merchant Services is an independent sales organization/agent; payment processing services are provided through third-party registered payment partners and their sponsor banks; the website is informational and marketing in nature. This is where you disclose the relationship structure generically without naming names. Flag for Soli: ask whether your agent agreement or card-brand rules require a specific registered-ISO disclosure line on your marketing site. Agent website disclosure requirements vary by ISO agreement, and this is the one item on this whole list that could conflict with your keep-bluu-invisible positioning. Better to know now than during a compliance sweep.

No guarantee of approval: pre-qualification is not an offer, approval, or commitment; all merchant accounts are subject to underwriting review by the processing partner and sponsor bank; eligibility answers do not create a contract. For a pharmacy audience that's been burned before, this honesty is also good marketing.

Any final relationship is governed by the merchant processing agreement executed with the processor/bank, and these site terms don't modify it.

### Content disclaimers (critical given your compliance-heavy blog strategy)

All content, including blog articles about LegitScript, NABP, VIRP, DEA, HIPAA, and licensing, is general information only and not legal, tax, regulatory, or compliance advice; readers should consult qualified counsel; regulations change and content may not reflect the most current requirements. Given that your content strategy is literally "demonstrate compliance fluency," this disclaimer is your seatbelt.

Rates, terms, savings estimates, and promotional offers are illustrative, subject to change, and subject to underwriting; actual pricing is set in the merchant agreement. This also covers the savings calculator on the restaurant/salon/dry-cleaner pages.

Third-party trademarks (LegitScript, NABP, Visa, Mastercard, card networks) belong to their owners; references don't imply endorsement or affiliation.

### User conduct and legal boilerplate

User representations: visitors submitting forms confirm the information is accurate and that they're authorized to act for the business; no unlawful use of the site; no submitting PHI, cardholder data, or credentials through any form on the site (mirrors the privacy policy).

Intellectual property: you own the site content, logo, and branding; limited license to view; no scraping, copying, or framing.

Disclaimer of warranties (site provided "as is"), limitation of liability, and indemnification.

Electronic communications consent (E-SIGN): user agrees that communications and any disclosures may be delivered electronically.

Governing law: Florida, with venue in Flagler County; whether to add arbitration and a class-action waiver is a genuine lawyer question, not a template decision.

Standard closers: severability, entire agreement, no waiver, right to modify terms with continued use as acceptance, right to terminate site access, and a contact block.
